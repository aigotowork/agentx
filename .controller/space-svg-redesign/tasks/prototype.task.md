## Author design

The review question is whether a visitor can locate a person by their work and recognize where to start a conversation, then return with their place and draft intact. A map of many small rooms would foreground navigation while shrinking the characters; a side-view lineup would enlarge them but weaken the sense of a place. The selected treatment is a two-room cutaway: the amber Signal Workshop and teal Glasshouse appear one at a time on a shared dark field desk. Their props, light, activity, and room silhouettes do the main work. Inked architecture carries stepped, mosaic-like character costumes to keep faces and names readable.

The Space page is composed of a masthead, RoomStage (room plate, artwork, actor targets and fixed talk tags), RoomDock (two locations and the People Index), and the disclosed ConversationLetter (portrait/header, working transcript, composer). The adjacent desktop letter becomes a full-height reader on narrow screens. Each station is a stable name/status target even when its actor moves; the visible moving body can also be picked. Selecting a person pins their pose and opens their letter without changing the room. On return, their target is still selected and the draft is retained by person. The index works across rooms and supplies the nonspatial keyboard path. Reduced motion holds figures at authored key poses and removes decorative cycles.
