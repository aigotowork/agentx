# Adapt a method

Read the selected source at a known revision and the consumer's actual scenario. Establish what judgment or outcome the source is meant to improve before translating its terms.

1. Extract its mechanism: inputs, decisions, intermediate results, feedback and stopping condition. Distinguish the author's claims from inspected implementation and observed results.
2. Map each needed concept, responsibility, tool and artifact to the consumer's existing owner. Mark absent counterparts and incompatible assumptions explicitly. Similar names do not prove equivalent semantics.
3. Decide separately what to preserve, adapt, reference or exclude. Preserve the useful causal relationship; remove imported delegation requirements, global modes and environment assumptions that do not apply. Existing user scope remains authoritative.
4. Write the smallest usable consumer flow with its necessary inputs, failure boundary and resource paths. Keep rationale and source revision in the owner’s design; keep instructions where consumers can discover them. Check permission and attribution for copied material.
5. Walk through a normal task and the mismatch most likely to break it. When execution is authorized, use an isolated real consumer and retain inputs, observations and discrepancies. A static walkthrough supports plausibility, not an effectiveness claim.

Revise the mapping when the trial exposes a mismatch. Stop once the selected outcome works within the stated limits; do not migrate unrelated frameworks, tests or historical evidence.
