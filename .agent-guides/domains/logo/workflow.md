# Work from the next design decision

Use for new identity work, substantial redesign or recovery after a narrow direction. Reuse an established brief for a bounded refinement. The stages below form an iterative working loop, not mandatory approval meetings. Continue authorized reversible work; consult the user only when a missing choice materially affects the result.

For phase-specific dialogue, non-specialist preference expression and next-round planning, compose [conversation and guidance](collaboration/conversation.md). Communication runs through the loop; it is not a single selection event.

## Select the mode

| Request | Start here | Avoid |
| --- | --- | --- |
| “Design a few logos” | Short brief and distinct territories | Automatically choosing the industry's default aesthetic |
| “More directions; these feel alike” | Reopen the concept and style matrix | More radii, palettes and finishes on one outline |
| “This direction works; make it elegant” | Identify what to retain, then vary the relevant structural levers | Replacing the accepted idea without explaining the consequence |
| “Like these references” | Extract liked traits and their source roles | Copying one distinctive commercial outline |
| “An engraved metal animal” | Subject, anatomy, engraving and material together | Substituting a flat generic icon and calling it finished |
| “Show these in use” | Presentation scenes with the real candidate versions | New artwork created only for attractive mockups |
| “Build the method/tools” | The appropriate method or architecture document | Drawing logos or implementing unrequested software |

## Form a brief without blocking progress

Inspect the real brand name, product or organization, available identity, relevant audience and major applications. Separate facts, the user's preferences, hypotheses and unknowns. Reuse session answers. When a useful choice is missing, ask one focused question and continue independent reference or structure work; do not turn a casual request into an intake form.

Capture what matters in the [brief record](collaboration/records.md): intended associations, undesirable associations, invariant requirements, style openness, application priorities, language/name requirements and the current deliverable. A generic sector such as SaaS is context, not art direction. If the user has not selected a style, keep more than one plausible expressive range open.

## Diverge in concepts before polishing

Use [research and inspiration](research/inspiration.md) to extract reference principles. Construct territories across several independent dimensions: meaning, subject, relationship, visual language, identity form, composition and material. Select combinations with a plausible connection to the brief; do not enumerate every Cartesian-product combination.

A direction should have a brief connection, a one-sentence proposition, a visible organizing idea, a distinction from its nearest alternative and the question this round should answer. Use the [direction record](collaboration/records.md); leave undecided facets open rather than inventing defaults. Examples:

- Resourcefulness expressed by a small workshop creature, drawn with rounded volume and an alert gesture.
- A system that grows, expressed as modular branches with crafted botanical detail.
- A signed toolmaker identity, expressed through an engraved monogram and distinctive lettering.

A direction can be abstract, figurative, typographic, narrative or ornamental. Use the [style atlas](craft/style-atlas.md) to find an untried formal language when candidates converge prematurely. Keep source and interpretation distinct; an agent's concept narrative is not market research.

Produce enough rough breadth to let the user compare different propositions. Scale the count to the request and the uncertainty. A small batch may be sufficient; an explicitly requested broad exploration merits multiple contact sheets. Do not inflate a direction count with recolors or tiny geometric edits. Reserve detailed construction for candidates whose premise is worth testing.

## Check whether the directions differ

Describe each candidate through its concept signature: semantic idea, subject, topology/relationship, contour grammar, identity form, composition and expressive medium. If only the color or roughness changed, label it as a variation. If the differences are mostly invisible without captions, reopen meaning, silhouette, subject or layout.

No fixed distance score establishes meaningful diversity. A pair of letter-based marks can be distinct through their construction and reading; a fox and a wolf can still be almost the same rounded-head solution. Compare what actually changes for the viewer.

Early boards should reveal alternatives fairly. Neutral versions help compare structure; colored and material boards are appropriate when those dimensions are the actual question. A black-and-white stage is useful for many tasks, not a universal prerequisite that erases a material-led brief.

## Select and converge

Apply [presentation and feedback](collaboration/presentation.md) together with [conversation and guidance](collaboration/conversation.md) when interpreting reactions or planning the next round. Seek feedback about a decision: which personality fits, what to retain, which association is wrong, or what combination is promising. Translate “rough,” “cool,” “premium” or “too generic” into hypotheses about form, detail, type, material and context; show a useful contrast instead of demanding specialist vocabulary.

Record a selected direction or continuing alternatives. Selection for development is not final approval, proof of effectiveness or legal clearance. Keep stable IDs and parent relationships; do not silently replace a previously chosen candidate.

## Develop the whole identifier

Refine subject structure, contour, internal detail, spacing, lettering and color through the applicable [construction methods](craft/construction.md). Explore material, illustration and motion where they contribute. Use geometry to control selected relationships; use optical changes where those improve the actual result.

Change related factors together when needed for the design. For diagnostic comparisons, control unrelated factors so the user can tell why it improved. A successful symbol still needs the real wordmark, lockup and relevant application treatment if those are part of the request.

## Choose what the next artifact must teach

Before spending effort on a polished board or prototype, name the material unknown and select an artifact capable of resolving it. Adapt the learning-goal mechanism in the [Design Kit source](research/sources.md) to the current scope; a full journey-map workshop is not a prerequisite for a logo refinement.

| Unknown | Useful next artifact | What it cannot establish alone |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Which proposition fits the intended brand? | Distinct territories with comparable maturity and concise intent | Audience recognition or production readiness |
| Whether roughness comes from contour or material | Controlled source comparison, then a corresponding finish study | A glossy mockup cannot isolate a hidden contour defect |
| Whether an ornate mark works in compact use | Actual-size comparison and a deliberate compact adaptation | Large-view attractiveness does not settle small-use behavior |
| Whether the workbench preserves mixed feedback | A complete interactive compare/annotate/continue path | A static gallery cannot establish persistence or interpretation correction |

Set fidelity by the question: rough construction can reveal a silhouette problem, while a material judgment needs a credible surface and lighting treatment. Preserve enough context for the judgment and avoid polishing unrelated details. The next result may also revise the question; record which evidence changed the frame instead of silently replacing the brief.

## Validate and deliver at the promised scope

Choose [evaluation](evaluation.md) and [presentation](collaboration/presentation.md) appropriate to the current stage. Concept exploration ends with credible alternatives and a clear next decision. A refinement ends with explained changes and relevant evidence. Production delivery needs editable sources, real variants and application checks. Do not imply that an exploratory board is deployment-ready.

When agent image inspection is unavailable or disallowed, generate the agreed artifacts, perform appropriate structural/rendering checks, invite user review and label visual judgment as unverified. Do not repeatedly request permission to read images. No methodological stage overrides the user's inspection policy.

## Recover from failure

| Signal | Change the work |
| --- | --- |
| Many candidates feel the same | Reopen semantic subjects, relationships and visual language; stop polishing one skeleton |
| “More professional” keeps returning | Check hierarchy, source craft, actual typography, anatomy, optical balance and application; do not add decorative grids as a substitute |
| Rich mockup hides a weak identifier | Present the source and an honest simplified/production rendering beside the effect |
| Simplification removes the liked character | Restore the recognition cue; simplify only for the relevant use |
| A tool is driving the aesthetic | Choose a medium that can realize the intended idea; disclose unavailable capability |
| Feedback conflicts | Separate brand-fit concerns, personal preference and application constraints; keep the trade-off visible |

End an iteration when it has answered its design question or exposed a specific remaining dependency. Do not generate another batch merely to appear productive.
