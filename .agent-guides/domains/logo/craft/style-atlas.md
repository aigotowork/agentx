# Style atlas

Use this atlas to expand or interpret art direction, not to select a fashionable default. A style is a set of visible decisions. “Premium,” “professional,” “cool” and “technology” need translation into contour, type, composition, detail, material and cultural context.

The seed [style vocabulary](../resources/style-vocabulary.yaml) owns reusable facet IDs and bilingual labels. It is a vocabulary, not an image library or a validated classifier. The recipes below explain craft and combinations; they do not define a closed list of styles.

## Keep the facets independent

A fox is a subject; rounded geometry is a visual language; subtractive arcs are a construction technique; metal is a material; an emblem is an identity form; alertness is an intended expression. Keeping these facets separate lets the user retain one quality while changing the rest. Do not use “fox-metal-premium-logo” as an indivisible style category.

| Family or direction | Formal decisions to explore | Reference needs and craft risks |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Geometric abstraction | Modules, angles, arcs, solids and negative space | Topology and optical mass; generic four-point stars and familiar app symbols |
| Rounded geometric animal | Anatomical masses, pose, curvature families, selective features | Actual animal anatomy; losing species or expression by rounding everything |
| Sculptural animal or object | Volume, planes, silhouette, undercuts and material | Viewpoint and lighting; polished surfaces cannot repair weak anatomy |
| Engraved / etched | Hatch direction, line spacing, dark-light hierarchy, edges | Engraving and print references; fine lines can fill or disappear in reduction |
| Woodcut / linocut | Bold carved planes, irregular edges, selective directional marks | Print process and black/white balance; arbitrary noise does not create craft |
| Hand-drawn / naive | Personal contour, unequal repetition, gesture and controlled imperfection | Observational drawing; a generic scribble may lack a distinctive subject |
| Brush / calligraphic | Stroke order, pressure, terminals and counter rhythm | Writing tradition and tool behavior; fake pressure effects can conflict with letter structure |
| Editorial / high-contrast lettering | Stroke contrast, sharp transitions, spacing and distinctive letter pairs | Actual name and language; thin details and poor kerning |
| Custom sans / modular lettering | Repeated skeleton, cuts, widths and intentional exceptions | Word rhythm and similarity; a typeface name alone is not a wordmark design |
| Blackletter / ornamental lettering | Dense texture, broken strokes, flourishes and reading rhythm | Script conventions and context; excessive ornament can obscure the name |
| Retro seal / workshop identity | Ring text, borders, secondary motifs and central hierarchy | Historical print/industry forms; invented establishment dates or empty claims |
| Heraldic / emblematic | Charge, shield, posture, framing and order of information | Appropriate cultural meanings; generic crest assembly or incompatible traditions |
| Folk / vernacular | Region-specific motifs, hand processes and pattern | Cultural provenance and current use; decontextualized symbols |
| Nouveau / organic ornament | Plant-like curves, interwoven contours and typographic unity | Period structure and rhythmic line; indiscriminate flourishes |
| Deco / architectural | Stepped proportions, long axes, fans and angular rhythm | Architecture and type; superficial gold decoration without a clear form |
| Mascot / character | Silhouette, facial structure, gesture, prop and personality | Expression and anatomy; dependence on tiny features for recognition |
| Narrative emblem | A concise scene with a dominant focal subject | Story and composition; equal-weight objects turn it into a crowded illustration |
| Isometric / spatial | Projection, consistent axes, occlusion and modular depth | Perspective and scale; inconsistent geometry or tiny windows doing all the work |
| Pixel / arcade | Chosen pixel grid, cluster rhythm, limited palette and deliberate jaggies | Pixel-native design; merely rasterizing a smooth shape loses the grammar |
| Sports / racing / esports | Forceful silhouette, motion, condensed type and outline hierarchy | Category comparison; generic shield/lightning motifs and aggressive overdetailing |
| Cybernetic / circuit | Trace logic, nodes, symmetry or controlled asymmetry | Mechanisms and visual hierarchy; decorative paths may overwhelm the identifier |
| Psychedelic / acid | Warped type, scale contrast, saturated color and rhythmic density | Letter readability and composition; unrelated effects piled together |
| Collage / editorial assemblage | Layer order, crops, cuts, printed texture and contrast | Constituent permissions and hierarchy; pasted assets without a unifying relation |
| Woven / knot / textile | Over-under order, repeated bands and border behavior | Textile logic; impossible crossings or a knot that becomes a blob |
| Botanical / ecological | Growth topology, asymmetry, leaf/branch morphology | Real plants and distinct abstraction; generic sprout symbolism |
| Liquid / inflatable | Surface tension, bulges, pinches, reflections and soft volume | Material behavior and silhouette; incidental blobs and excessive highlights |
| Architectural / spatial monogram | Openings, plans, negative space and structural rhythm | The actual letter or spatial idea; decorative building details without identity |
| Variable / generative family | A shared invariant and controlled transformation | Rules, bounds and static states; random variants with no consistent recognition cue |

These families can overlap. An engraved animal emblem and a rounded metal animal may share a subject while differing in edge grammar, interior detail, composition and medium. A hybrid should have a governing language; list what the secondary language contributes.

## Materials are designable, not magic filters

| Expression | Art direction and implementation | Preserve in the source system |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Engraved metal | Incised grooves, directional hatching or recessed relief; coherent side lighting and grain | Shape hierarchy and readable contours; distinguish actual hatching from a photo mockup |
| Polished / brushed metal | Surface roughness, environment reflections, bevel profile, highlight width | Underlying planes and silhouette; choose vector gradients, 3D or generated raster according to fidelity |
| Cast metal / enamel | Solid border, separate material wells, manufacturing-like thickness | Shape separations and planned one-color treatment when relevant |
| Emboss / deboss | Raised or recessed edges tied to paper/light behavior | An emboss mask or relief source; avoid claiming a shadow image is an editable stamping master |
| Glass / translucent | Thickness, refraction, overlapping color and background interaction | Foreground-background behavior and intended delivery background |
| Textile / stitched patch | Thread direction, seam hierarchy, border and simplified interior details | Production line spacing and small detail limits for the intended process |
| Ink / print texture | Ink gain, paper relationship, edge texture and limited color separation | Clean source plus intentional texture layers where useful |
| Holographic / iridescent | View-dependent color relationship and restrained highlights | Stable identifying contour and an honest static rendering |
| Luminous / neon | Bright core, glow falloff and dark-surround behavior | A usable source shape and support for contexts where glow is inappropriate |

A metal treatment can be a presentation finish, a primary brand expression or an exploration aid. Record the actual role. Never claim a flat gradient is a physically accurate metal render, or require metal briefs to use a gradient when a richer medium is available.

## Worked direction recipes

**Rounded geometric animal:** pick a meaningful species and pose; study head/body masses; decide the recognition cues; draw a silhouette; construct selected curves with a coherent radius family; preserve intentional asymmetry and expression; refine the actual wordmark; then explore flat, enamel or metal expression. Test whether it still reads as the intended animal. Avoid starting from identical circles and hoping anatomy emerges.

**Engraved toolmaker identity:** select an object, monogram or creature appropriate to the brief; establish a strong contour and internal planes; place hatching along those planes; vary density to preserve focal hierarchy; design the type and framing; compare an etched rendering, a clean line version and a relevant application. Do not add fictitious dates, awards or origin claims.

**Expressive character identity:** define a personality through action rather than adjectives; sketch multiple poses and silhouettes; choose one expressive cue; develop face, anatomy and props; relate the wordmark; derive compact identifiers only for actual uses. A character may become a primary mark or a supporting asset; that remains a design decision.

**Lettering with material character:** develop distinctive structure for the real name before applying relief or surface treatment. Draw key letter pairs, correct spacing, preserve intentional irregularity, then match highlights or carved edges to the strokes. A licensed font can be a starting point; its license and modifications must fit delivery.

## Interpret style preferences

When the user chooses a reference, ask or infer which facet is liked and retain the evidence. Offer a discriminating next comparison: same animal in engraved and rounded languages, or the same metal language applied to a monogram and a creature. Do not assume preference for a sample transfers to every facet.

When the user asks for broader inspiration, change several concept dimensions deliberately. When refining a chosen style, preserve its governing grammar and target the weak relationships. A style label is a route to craft, not an instruction to copy a preset.
