# Research and inspiration

Use when the brief is open, references are supplied, a style needs interpretation or alternatives feel repetitive. Research should change a design decision. Stop collecting when the next comparison has a defensible basis; an enormous moodboard is not evidence of an original concept.

## Give each reference a role

| Role | What to extract | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Meaning | A relationship or metaphor | A loom coordinates independent threads |
| Subject | Anatomy, mechanism, object or scene | The cheek, ear placement and posture of an alert animal |
| Formal | Contour, proportion, rhythm or negative space | Broad body masses with narrow connecting gaps |
| Stylistic | An expressive grammar | Dense hatching, irregular ink edges, restrained two-color printing |
| Material | Light and surface behavior | Brushed grain, recessed engraving, enamel and metal borders |
| Application | How an identity occupies a real context | Embroidered badge, app header, book spine or packaging seal |
| Category comparison | Existing identifiers near this use | Similar animals, letter combinations or badge structures to distinguish from |

One image can have multiple roles, but identify which properties are being used. A photo of brass explains material; it does not license copying an engraved crest. A generated image can be a style hypothesis; it cannot be evidence of a real brand's practice.

Retain the source link or local asset, creator when known, date/version where relevant, role, extracted traits and permitted use. Mark rights as unknown when unknown. Looking at a reference and incorporating its pixels or distinctive drawing are different uses. Do not claim clearance from an image search or a permissive model label.

## Search beyond logo galleries

Seek references in relevant product history and category identities, then look outside the category: tools, architecture, mechanisms, botany, animals, weaving, maps, games, printing, calligraphy, sculpture, scientific imagery or vernacular objects. Use sources that supply the actual needed property rather than collecting fashionable finished logos.

Construct queries from role + subject + property + medium. For a rounded animal identity, separate “animal posture and head anatomy,” “geometric animal negative space,” and “cast metal surface under side light.” The first informs structure, the second abstraction, and the third finish. Keep those sources separate in the design record.

When no reference tools are available, use project-owned/user-provided sources, clearly labeled self-drawn studies, or reasoned hypotheses. Do not fabricate external moodboards, creators or source access. If browsing a large library is future work, describe it as a proposal rather than inventing present coverage.

## Extract a principle before drawing

Write a short causal note: “Wide head, small muzzle and low detail create a soft, approachable character; this candidate will preserve the ears and posture so it still reads as the intended animal.” Treat the perceived quality as a hypothesis until reviewed.

Translate a source through operations such as simplify, rotate, mirror, crop, interlock, repeat, compress, exaggerate, substitute, unfold or change viewpoint. Explain what was preserved and transformed. Collage can be a legitimate exploratory and final method when source permissions support it; trace each constituent. Combining several copied logos does not make the result original.

For cross-domain transfer, first name the desired quality, then find a source where a concrete relationship produces it. Transfer that relationship into a new proposition and state what the next comparison could reveal. “Borrow the welcoming rhythm of a workshop's hand-lettered signs” is more usable than “make it look like this brand.” Check both fit and a counterexample: the same irregularity may feel warm in a maker's mark and careless in precision lettering. This is a local adaptation of Design Kit's Mash-Ups mechanism; the [source ledger](sources.md) states the inspected scope.

Use cultural references with their actual context. Avoid treating indigenous, sacred or community-specific symbols as generic texture. When context is material and unavailable, choose a better-understood source or leave that interpretation unresolved.

## Use a morphological matrix

Choose the dimensions that could break the current limitation. The following is a working space, not a compulsory list or random generator.

| Dimension | Possible values |
| --- | --- |
| Intended expression | Inventive, calm, precise, exuberant, resilient, artisanal, mysterious |
| Semantic relationship | Joining, growing, revealing, navigating, guarding, transforming, orbiting |
| Subject | Letter, creature, plant, tool, landscape, architecture, invented object |
| Identity form | Symbol, wordmark, monogram, emblem, character, combination, variable family |
| Visual language | Geometric, gestural, editorial, engraved, folk, pixel, ornamental, sculptural |
| Composition | Axial, radial, interlocking, layered, asymmetric, framed, scene-based |
| Material or finish | Flat ink, relief, metal, enamel, paper, glass, textile, luminous, liquid |

Select combinations because they support a concept. For “resourceful builder,” a rounded mechanical creature, an engraved toolmaker monogram and a modular city are plausible different territories. Keep combinations legible; mixing many stylistic systems without hierarchy often weakens rather than enriches the idea.

## Reframe when all alternatives share one assumption

When a concrete current solution has become a trap, ask what brand purpose or intended association it serves. Find alternative ways to serve that same purpose, then turn promising alternatives into drawable propositions. For example, an interlocking X may serve “coordinated work”; weaving, a workshop creature and a toolmaker ligature can test other expressions of coordination. Reject branches that only rename the X or change its finish.

Keep the climb grounded in the brief. If the purpose becomes “deliver value” and no useful visual siblings follow, the reframing failed; return to a specific situation or use analogy instead. Record the useful branches, the repeated assumption and why a cosmetic branch was pruned. Do not require fixed branch counts or an extra user decision gate. This adapts Concept Fan, attributed in the [source ledger](sources.md#method-and-skill-references); its upstream “direction” terminology maps to the shared purpose here, not a second definition of our direction record. A blank-page task or an accepted mark needing spacing correction does not require this technique.

## Ways to generate a different kind of idea

- **Analogy:** map the product's organizing relationship to another domain. Coordination can become weaving, a workshop or an ecosystem.
- **Attribute transfer:** give a letter the posture of a creature or a mechanical part the warmth of a handmade object.
- **Figure-ground reversal:** let the absence carry a second reading; verify that the primary reading survives.
- **Narrative compression:** preserve the decisive gesture of a scene in an emblem or character, rather than illustrating every step.
- **Material-first exploration:** start from a casting, engraving or folded surface and discover a form suited to that medium.
- **Lettering-first exploration:** use distinctive letter pairs, ligatures, language structure and rhythm as the subject.
- **Constraint inversion:** if all work is symmetric, test a balanced asymmetric mark; if all work is flat, test volume or tactile expression.
- **Family generation:** define a shared contour or module rule that supports several related identifiers when the brand actually needs variation.

Choose the operation that addresses the current repetitive pattern. A worksheet filled with eight headings is not evidence that the thinking diversified.

## A usable moodboard

Group sources by a proposed territory and name the question they answer. Include a short note about extracted traits and at least the material needed to distinguish the territory from its nearest alternative. Do not let one visually dominant commercial logo become the hidden template for the entire board.

Separate source material, self-drawn studies and candidates in the layout. Annotate uncertain tags rather than presenting model or agent classifications as facts. If the user chooses references, preserve which exact properties they liked; liking the color of a metallic fox does not imply selecting a fox or metal as the new brand.

End with territories the agent can draw, a source basis, known uncertainties and a specific next comparison. Record these using [records](../collaboration/records.md); use the [style atlas](../craft/style-atlas.md) for technique selection.
