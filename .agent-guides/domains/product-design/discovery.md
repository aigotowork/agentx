# Discover the problem and next useful evidence

Use when the people, situation, problem or value behind an idea is uncertain. An established behavior defect can go directly to its contract and affected scenario. Start with the existing request and material; a user instruction supplies direction and authority, but does not fabricate market or usability evidence.

## Separate experience from explanation

Retain one identifiable experience: participant role or source, relevant time/context, trigger, goal, actions, workaround, outcome and material location. Distinguish recalled statements from observed behavior and system logs. Record the permitted use of actual research material with its owner.

| Layer | Question | Illustrative equipment-loan example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Experience | What happened or was said? | A coordinator asked two people who held a projector |
| Interpretation | What might explain it? | Availability was invisible, or handovers were never recorded |
| Need | What outcome matters in that situation? | Confirm that equipment can be collected before leaving |
| Solution | What intervention could meet it? | A shared list, custodian confirmation or a scanning workflow |
| Decision | What do we choose, and what could change it? | Observe handover recording before investing in scanning |

The example is invented. Keep actual material separate from hypotheses, teaching cases and Agent-generated personas. Several copies of one complaint are one source, not independent corroboration.

## Choose evidence by the decision

| Unknown | Suitable method | Boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Circumstances, motivation, current alternatives | Interview about a recent specific experience | Recall or stated willingness is not observed use |
| Actual work, interruptions and omitted steps | Contextual observation and existing artifacts | One session cannot establish prevalence |
| Where failures cluster | Support records, search terms or relevant logs | Missing instrumentation and silent users remain absent; frequency alone does not explain cause |
| Population prevalence | Defined sampling frame and consistent measurement | Convenience samples do not establish a population percentage |
| Candidate comprehension and task completion | Neutral task in a representative prototype | Does not prove long-term adoption, persistence or business benefit |

Ask a question that could change a decision: “Was the result unavailable, or was the handover never recorded?” Ask what happened next and what records exist. Avoid suggesting the desired solution in the question. Group findings by circumstances and obstacles; preserve contrary examples. Choose participants for differences relevant to the task, including failure and accessibility conditions, rather than inventing demographic personas.

## Explain progress and compare opportunities

Use Jobs to Be Done when the unresolved question is why people switch, stay or abandon alternatives. Restore the specific trigger, prior approach, search, concerns, choice and outcome. Describe the progress sought in that circumstance, including functional, emotional or social factors only where evidence supports them. A job should still exist if the proposed product vanished; a button click is an implementation, not that job.

Use an opportunity-solution tree when several plausible paths compete for one outcome. Separate the desired outcome, evidenced needs/obstacles, candidate interventions and assumptions to test. Edges express proposed contribution, not proof. Compare opportunities before estimating one imagined implementation; an expensive first solution does not make the underlying problem unimportant.

For each candidate, identify what must be true, supporting and opposing evidence, consequence if wrong, and the smallest discriminating observation. Compare problem value, comprehension, technical feasibility and operational viability only where they affect this decision. Select the consequential uncertainty that can actually be investigated; a numerical risk score is not calibrated evidence.

Write a bounded problem statement: who, under which trigger and constraints, seeks what progress, encounters which obstacle with the current approach, and suffers what consequence; link evidence and unknowns. Hand [requirements](requirements.md) the selected opportunity, intended result, constraints, alternatives and reasons, with unresolved assumptions visible.

Stop when evidence supports the current reversible decision and no unresolved constraint immediately invalidates it. Costly commitments may need stronger evidence. Do not use a fixed interview count or tag-saturation threshold as universal sufficiency. An Agent may organize sources, expose contradictions and propose research; simulated interviews never add participants.

Further method sources: [GOV.UK user needs](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/start-by-learning-user-needs), [in-depth interviews](https://www.gov.uk/service-manual/user-research/using-in-depth-interviews), [Christensen Institute JTBD](https://www.christenseninstitute.org/theory/jobs-to-be-done/), [ProductTalk opportunity trees](https://www.producttalk.org/opportunity-solution-trees/). The working method above is a synthesis, not a claim of observed effectiveness in the current project.
