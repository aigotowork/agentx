# Materials and composition

Use for technical explanations and related feature demonstrations or promotional media. [Expression design](../planning/expression-design.md) chooses the understanding experience. This method chooses material roles and composes the finished artifact. It does not supply a complete marketing strategy, brand system, video toolchain or renderer.

## Separate purpose, material and delivery

State who encounters the content, what they should notice, understand, feel or do, then design the observations and actions that support it. Select materials, compose a finished work and prepare only required channel versions. These are distinct decisions: video is time-based presentation, a model describes relationships, and an iframe is a hosting boundary.

| Material | Useful role | Establish before use |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual screen/terminal/device capture | Show an object, action, feedback or result | Product/version, starting state and readable framing |
| Edited audiovisual demonstration | Focus attention on an experience or mechanism | Scene purpose and which scenes show facts versus concepts |
| Short loop or local motion | Show one nearby change with low navigation effort | Clear start/end, pause needs and whether timing/sound matters |
| Annotated image, photo or illustration | Locate, compare, establish context or visual identity | Actual versus illustrative content, crop and target size |
| Structural model | Expose responsibility, causal rule, boundary or state | Objects, edges, viewpoint and conditions |
| Data/table/calculation | Compare quantities and reveal thresholds | Source/assumptions, units, scales and comparable conditions |
| Code, diff, log or event trace | Locate the mechanism in implementation | Critical slice, input/output and actual versus teaching execution |
| Runnable work | Let readers use and vary a rule | Task loop, operations, rules and interpretable feedback |
| Voice, interview, music or sound | Explain timing, direct attention or express experience | Attribution, relationship to claims and access without sound |
| Downloadable decision card, data or project | Support later application or reproduction | Usage conditions, source/version and agreement with the explanation |

Titles, captions, cover frames, transitions and navigation also serve useful roles. Each asset can contribute explanation, evidence, context, emotion or orientation; every asset need not prove a technical claim. Select needed materials rather than using every category. A short loop can be video or another supported format; “animated” does not mean GIF.

## Design the finished viewing path

Choose the path by purpose. A tutorial needs starting position, action, feedback and continuation. A feature introduction can reveal the problem/result, key operation and distinguishing outcome. Promotion can establish relevance, representative experience, supported value and a next step. A technical explanation can show an observation, pause/emphasize it, compare mechanisms and vary a condition. Do not force every short video into a paper or use promotional pacing to hide required evidence.

Before detailed production, use the least elaborate storyboard or previsualization that resolves the current uncertainty. Identify scenes consistently within the work. For each consequential scene state the viewer question, visible objects/state, action/change, framing/emphasis, continuity from the preceding scene, narration/sound if needed and expected observation. Timing estimates guide production; actual playback is needed to assess pacing.

Crop and zoom to make important actions readable while retaining location. Place annotations at the relevant time without covering evidence. Transitions should preserve spatial/object identity or clearly signal a stage change. Remove irrelevant waits but leave enough time to recognize the result. A timeline, caption and scene can project the same model; they must agree about names, states and direction.

## Capture actual behavior reproducibly

For each shot, connect claim → source/version → initial state → action → business-ready signal → visible result → viewing hold → reset. Separate genuine recording, controlled capture with fixed data or simulated dependencies, synthetic illustration and mixed composition. Label distinctions where they change the viewer's interpretation.

Business readiness and viewer reading time differ. Observe the meaningful result, such as a persisted record or state transition, then allow the intended viewer time to inspect it. A fixed sleep, network-idle signal or completed automation assertion cannot alone determine both. A fresh browser context isolates browser storage; it does not reset a database or external account. Use an owned reset procedure for the actual resources involved.

Capture the needed tasks and states rather than recording an entire session by default. Retake a failed or unclear shot after restoring its starting state. Preserve the causal relationship through edits. Disclose speed changes, skipped steps, simulated responses or conceptual overlays when they could change a claim about timing or behavior. Edited duration is not measured latency. A simulated provider response supports controlled interface behavior, not that provider's real speed or reliability.

Use public-safe content and identities. Recordings, traces, HAR files, logs and authentication state are different artifacts; removing private data from one does not clean the others. Follow actual task authority for live effects and keep each source in its existing owner.

## Produce images and time-based media for their roles

For images or a series, define the focal object, necessary identity/geometry, emphasis, text space and what may vary. Analyze a reference by its purpose and visual mechanism before borrowing surface appearance. Keep exact technical text editable rather than relying on generated lettering. When aspect ratio changes, recompose if cropping would remove the action or evidence.

Use a representative frame and a difficult view/aspect to resolve important production uncertainty before refining every variant. Then check the series together for object identity, color meaning and continuity; one successful image does not validate the set.

For edits, keep the original and an editable working source. Correct global framing/tone before local detail where appropriate, preserve factual boundaries and compare the relevant before/after. Inspect final-size readability and relevant fine detail: edges, distorted geometry, repeated cloning, halos, damaged labels and inconsistent object identity. Generated or repaired features cannot become evidence that the real product has them. Provenance helps identify a process; it does not prove the depicted claim.

Choose a loop for one short change when precise seeking or sound is unnecessary. Choose controlled video for multiple phases, pause/revisit, narration or accurate temporal comparison. Choose a still for a fixed state. Compare clarity, file size, loading and playback in the actual host; a GIF is not automatically lightweight. Provide pause/reduced-motion behavior and a poster/keyframe or equivalent route where needed.

Use narration, captions, sound and music to coordinate explanation and attention. Essential visual information needs a visual transcript or description where necessary; subtitles alone may omit it. Essential audio information needs a usable alternative. Check the soundless viewing path and, when the audio track carries a separate explanation, the audio-only path.

## Keep a small material plan

Keep this in the existing [brief](../planning/article-brief.md), not another asset-management system:

| Decision | Record |
| --- | --- |
| Audience, placement and role | Main artifact, explanation, evidence, orientation or takeaway; intended result |
| Essential content | Objects, actions, states, data, words and sound that must be perceivable |
| Composition | Capture/model/motion combination; scene or keyframe sequence and holds |
| Sources and editable work | Product/data version, original media, rights/attribution and work still to create |
| Delivery variants | Actual destinations, dimensions/aspect, locale/theme, audio and controls |
| Alternative and maintenance | Preview, keyframes, transcript, independent launch and invalidating changes |

Associate source recordings, scripts, model source, project, data and narration with exports such as compressed video, captions, loops and cover frames. One compressed output is not the entire maintainable source. Store actual media in the content/production owner's established home, not this guidance directory. Preserve external image, music, font, quotation and participant use conditions appropriate to the material.

## Review the actual composition

Locate findings by scene, timestamp, object or source location. Check that the audience can find the point and continue, that emphasis and holds serve observation, that actual/simulated/conceptual/measured facts are distinct, and that every representation refers to the same relevant version and rule.

Inspect each required channel version in its real dimensions, audio mode and controls. A feed entry may lack the article's context; a mobile crop may remove the target; a poster may make a stronger claim than the video. Reframe or supply context when necessary. Do not generate unused variants or require a fixed number of rehearsals, art directions or sign-offs.

A polished composition can add visual and emotional value. It still needs its promised task, truthful claims and [actual delivery checks](../publication/renderer-and-release.md). Source inspection and storyboard reasoning alone do not establish successful playback, production quality or audience effects.
