# Method basis and limits

The domain is an authored synthesis of reader-oriented technical explanation, claim/evidence analysis, explicit model views and teaching experiments. It was adapted from the xsaas technical-blog method and selected expression, materials and executable-work research into a portable form. The package supplies instructions and a constructed example; it supplies no external Skill snapshot, runtime, renderer or measured learning result.

The following references identify method influences and further reading. They are optional background, not hidden prerequisites or activated instructions. Upstream files can have different scope, permissions, licenses and technical assumptions; selecting a tool or redistributing its material requires its actual contract.

| Reference | Useful question | Adoption boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [Diátaxis](https://diataxis.fr/) | Is the reader learning, acting, understanding or consulting a reference? | Use the need distinction; classification alone does not establish quality and explanation need not be a prose essay |
| [C4 diagram review](https://c4model.com/diagrams/checklist) | Are scope, elements, labels and relationships interpretable? | Model views require actual semantics and evidence; diagrams do not establish runtime guarantees |
| [W3C non-text content guidance](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/non-text-content.html) | Which information needs an equivalent accessible route? | A fallback is one concern; it is not a complete accessibility conformance claim |
| [Toulmin, The Uses of Argument](https://doi.org/10.1017/CBO9780511840005) | What connects the claim, grounds, conditions and challenge? | The adapted technical examples are authored; no empirical effect or scoring model is derived from the citation |
| [Mayer, Multimedia Learning](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316941355) | How do representations, attention and processing demands relate to learning? | Research entry only; this domain does not claim its specific artifact designs have been empirically validated |
| [Gries, dispersion research](https://doi.org/10.1075/ijcl.13.4.02gri) | What can linguistic distribution describe? | Frequency/distribution can locate review candidates; no linguistic formula or automatic quality score is adopted |

## What the method establishes

Design reasoning supports a plausible workflow under explicit assumptions. Static walkthroughs support understandable decisions in their supplied cases. Link/metadata checks support specific structural properties. Actual rendering, real integration, reader comprehension and operational outcomes require their own evidence.

No media form is universally best. A runnable work can expose necessary experience, a static trace can explain a fixed case, and precise text can support a complex conditional argument. Compare forms against the same reader task and important facts. Revise the method when actual use reveals a wrong assumption, keeping claims about benefit within the observed context.
