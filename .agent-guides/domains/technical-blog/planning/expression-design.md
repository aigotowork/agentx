# Design the explanation before prose

Use for substantive content and consequential changes of form. Describe what the reader can currently do, where they are blocked and what they should be able to explain, predict, operate or choose afterward. These are task descriptions, not measured quantities of knowledge.

## Expose the decisive relationship

Follow reader goal → concrete obstacle → relationship that must be visible → plausible forms → observation/action sequence. Revisit the relationship if the selected form still requires the reader to reconstruct the decisive behavior mentally.

| Obstacle | Candidate form | Added visibility | Main risk |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Interleaved states are hard to track | Steppable events or timeline | Current state beside each actor's saved observation | Too many controls or hidden event ordering |
| Changed conditions have unclear consequences | Calculator, parameter explorer or small app | Input, rule, result and boundary | A black box with unexplained output |
| An operation depends on location and feedback | Annotated steps or short video | Starting position, action, visible response and continuation | Version drift or edits hiding important waits |
| Fixed alternatives need simultaneous comparison | Table, aligned views or small multiples | Differences under the same conditions | Inconsistent labels/scales or unnecessary motion |
| Responsibility or topology is unclear | Layered structural views | Boundaries and collaboration | Essential paths buried in disclosure or occluded in 3D |
| Intermediate change matters | Staged demonstration or migration explorer | Mixed states, compatibility and recovery | Showing only successful endpoints |
| Conditions and derivation need scrutiny | Precise prose, formulas and decision tables | Assumptions, logical bridge and limits | Compression removing a necessary premise |
| A judgment needs practice | Challenge or small game with feedback | Prediction, consequence and strategy adjustment | Rewarding speed or completion without understanding |

This table supplies candidates, not an automatic choice or hierarchy. One figure and a few paragraphs can suffice. A runnable work can carry the central explanation. Use multiple media when they add complementary observations or useful comparison, and explain the contribution of repeated representations.

## Compare forms at sufficient fidelity

For a consequential choice, sketch plausible alternatives under the same reader, task and necessary truth conditions. Compare whether the key relationship is visible, the prerequisite/interface burden, control over sequence and pace, ability to revisit, accessibility, loading, production and maintenance. Implementing two finished versions is unnecessary. An obviously suitable simple text answer can state its reason directly.

Prefer a form that preserves important facts and outcomes while reducing tracking or operation burden without introducing a material disadvantage. Where factors conflict, record the trade-off. Important content can justify substantial development if it creates necessary observation or experience. Neither minimum cost, longer prose nor richer media is a universal winner.

## Script the discovery opportunity

Design what the reader will notice, rather than promising surprise. Place old observation beside durable state, change one decisive condition, compare mechanisms under equivalent inputs or reveal a failure boundary. Do not manufacture a reversal by hiding a premise.

Before outlining prose, write a short sequence:

| Moment | Reader sees/does | Observation | Explanation's job |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Orient | Inspect named objects and initial values | Scope and constraints are clear | Supply only necessary prerequisites |
| Predict or question | Consider the consequential change | The reader's current expectation becomes examinable | Frame the observation; a quiz control is optional |
| Reveal | Step, inspect or watch the decisive event | A relevant difference becomes visible | Connect the observation to the rule |
| Compare | Apply the same conditions to another mechanism | Which rule changes the outcome is clear | Preserve common assumptions and explain cost |
| Transfer | Change a condition or inspect a new case | The rule can be used beyond one preset | State limits and remaining real-system questions |

This sequence can become interaction, a paused demonstration or keyframes. Supply prose, captions, narration and optional depth around its observations. Let readers reach explanations directly; compulsory answers and progress tracking are unnecessary unless the actual learning task calls for them.

For procedures, cut steps by meaningful state change, including start, action, feedback and continuation. Choose video when continuous change matters, steps when locating and revisiting matter. For full use of a mechanism, select [executable explanations](../production/executable-explanations.md). Plan supporting materials through [materials and composition](../production/materials-and-composition.md).

## Preserve access and evaluate the result

Provide an unavailable-medium route carrying the central relation: keyframes with events, a relationship summary, representative inputs/outputs or a visual transcript. State its limited exploration. A human fallback does not establish a complete search or Markdown projection; [renderer checks](../publication/renderer-and-release.md) own that question.

Review where the relationship is actually observable, which effort remains necessary and whether a changed case can be reasoned about. Adding length, movement or decorative controls alone does not add explanatory value. Removing a decisive state is harmful even when the composition looks simpler. Moving a failure from before commit to after commit must change the shown outcome when the rules require it.

Without actual reader observation, report a designed opportunity for understanding. Prediction, boundary identification and transfer on a new case can reveal misunderstanding; liking a demo, time on page and an intended “aha” do not by themselves demonstrate learning. Keep any effect claim within the observed task and readers.
