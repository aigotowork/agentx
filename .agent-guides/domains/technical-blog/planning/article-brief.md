# Explanation brief

Use this scaffold for substantial content. Keep it in the owning Task or research note, combine related prompts and omit irrelevant work. Replace prompts with actual decisions before calling the brief ready. A bounded spelling correction reuses the existing argument.

## Reader and argument

- Working title, primary reader and triggering situation.
- Decision or action the reader should be able to make; its relation to any series.
- Necessary prior knowledge and definitions supplied locally.
- Central claim or relationship, plausible alternative, conditions and scope exclusions.
- Transferable result and the distinction from existing reference or procedural documentation.

## Technical basis

| Claim or uncertainty | Native/primary basis and source state | Decisive challenge or observation | Allowed conclusion and remaining limit |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Replace with a consequential claim | Give a resolvable source and relevant version or inspection state | State what could change the conclusion | Distinguish inspected, measured, proposed and unknown |

Identify actors, objects, identities, responsibility boundaries, meaningful states and invariants. Define notation and units. Mark simplifications and any divergence from the actual implementation. Apply [research and evidence](research-and-evidence.md) to claims and comparisons.

## Primary form and experience

- The reader's obstacle and the relationship or change that must become observable.
- Chosen form and reason; a plausible alternative and trade-off when the choice matters. A sketch is enough.
- Intended discovery and a changed case the reader could predict or handle. An intended insight is not an observed learning result.
- Observation/action sequence before prose, with keyframes or a storyboard when needed.
- Supporting text, captions, local interpretation and optional depth.
- Access to the central model when the primary medium cannot run or play; its limits.

| Reader question or moment | What the reader sees/does | New observation | Explanation or consequence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Replace with an actual question | View, action, scene or worked example | Name the visible relationship | State the inference it supports |

For each visual, specify scope and edge/encoding meaning. For an experiment, include its [teaching contract](../production/interactive-experiments.md#specify-the-teaching-contract). For a dedicated runnable work, connect concept → implementation rule → reader action → observation and state its necessary task loop, version and [hosting boundary](../production/executable-explanations.md#choose-the-hosting-boundary). For procedural media, include starting state, action, visible result and continuation condition.

Keep the [material plan](../production/materials-and-composition.md#keep-a-small-material-plan) here: asset role, essential content, source/version, capture or production, editable source, required delivery variants, fallback and refresh trigger. Scenes can name footage/graphic, change, emphasis/narration, continuity and intended observation. A related promotion states its audience response and next step without imitating an argumentative article.

## Delivery and acceptance

- Selected source and actual renderer capabilities; named missing implementation work.
- Public identity, locale, author, dates and tags using the consumer's real schema.
- Human fallback, keyboard/mobile/reduced-motion needs and actual machine discovery/text policy.
- Decisive cases and checks with expected results; distinguish plans from execution.
- Source/version changes that invalidate the explanation, ownership and correction path.
- Unknowns that block the thesis, artifact or publication.

A ready brief lets its implementer explain the reader's gain, investigate the claim, build the views and identify unverified behavior without guessing at rules. “Add rich diagrams and a demo” does not specify a production-ready explanation.
