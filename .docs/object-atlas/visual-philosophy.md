# Signal Engraving

Treat a system as a plate engraved for repeated consultation. A warm mineral ground carries fine ruled traces, graphite boundaries and carefully registered stamps. Texture belongs to the paper; the information remains clean. Every intersection, fold and endpoint should feel deliberately cut.

Use scale to establish gravity. A broad sheet of quiet construction lines surrounds a few compact marks; one field of sulphur yellow pulls the eye into the composition. Rich indigo and oxide remain legible at a glance. The active mark should look like a hand-placed register, an effect that depends on exact spacing and restraint.

Let shape speak before lettering. A lozenge, a square and an open diamond distinguish three kinds of presence. Repeated ports and small breaks establish rhythm. Engraving quality lies in the consistency of those small marks and in the deliberate exceptions.

Set lettering into the geometry. A heavy title names the plate; a small monospaced face measures and identifies. Run inscriptions along their line of force and leave a deliberate patch of ground behind each one. Use words where a direction or a distinction would otherwise be lost.

Leave an unbroken path for the eye to retrace. Dashed lines mark a weaker kind of link, and arrows stop precisely at their destinations. The sheet should reward both immediate recognition and a painstaking second look: the same composition, read at two different speeds.
